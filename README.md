## Abstract

The origin of the Moon remains one of the open questions of planetary science.
The canonical giant impact model predicts neither the near-isotopic identity of Earth and Moon,
nor the crustal dichotomy, nor the ≈ 300 Myr delay of the terrestrial dynamo.

This work proposes an alternative grounded in a necessary thermodynamic observation:
any Earth-mass planet emerges from accretion in a state of near-total melting
(E_grav exceeds the mantle fusion energy by a factor of 155).
The proto-Earth is therefore a rapidly rotating magma sphere (T_rot ≈ 5 h),
without a stabilizing satellite, evolving in an extreme Hadean environment —
silicate atmosphere at 2,000–4,000 K, T-Tauri Sun, active residual bombardment —
that maintains the system out of equilibrium for 30 to 50 million years.

In this context, Moon formation results from a **Triple Phase Transition** driven by
the progressive segregation of Fe-Ni. The chaotic wobble of the rotation axis in the
body frame — sustained by continuous Hadean perturbations — generates toroidal flows
that enter **elliptical parametric resonance** with the inertial modes of the fluid
(Malkus 1968, Kerswell 2002, Lacaze 2004). The resulting growth rate σ_R = Ωf/2
brings the Coherent Magmatic Torus (CMT) to the bifurcation velocity
U_crit ≈ 9.8 km/s in ≈ 32 hours.

Three coupled transitions produce N = 2–3 cohesive ejection episodes in 3–4 weeks
and a Hadean dynamo delay of 290–360 Myr (Tarduno et al. 2015, 2025).
The central prediction — seismic interface at d ≈ 200–315 km,
impedance contrast |R| ∈ [0.01; 0.04] — is testable by **Chang'e 7**
(lunar south pole, August 2026) and **Artemis III** (2028–2029).

---

## The three coupled transitions

1. **Rheological** — the silicate melt acquires a yield stress (Bingham-Herschel law)
   and organises a coherent toroidal flow in the intertropical belt |φ| < 30°: the CMT.
2. **Mechanical** — elliptical parametric resonance drives the CMT to bifurcation velocity,
   producing 2–3 cohesive ejection episodes beyond the Roche radius in 3–4 weeks.
3. **Magnetic** — the terrestrial dynamo emerges ≈ 300 Myr after the ejections cease,
   consistent with Jack Hills zircon palaeomagnetic data.
