## Abstract

This work proposes a theory of lunar formation grounded in a thermodynamic necessity:
any Earth-mass planet emerges from accretion in a state of near-total silicate mantle melting.
Accretion energy exceeds total fusion energy by a factor of 155 — the proto-Earth could only
have been a rapidly rotating magma sphere immersed in a Hadean inferno: a raging T-Tauri Sun,
a silicate atmosphere at 2,000–4,000 K, incessant planetesimal bombardment, still-active
short-lived radionuclides, and a chaotic axial obliquity between 40° and 70°.

It is in this context — and nowhere else — that the **Triple Phase Transition (TPT)** is
triggered. Its unique driver is the **progressive Fe-Ni segregation**, which simultaneously
governs three regime changes:

1. **Rheological transition** — the silicate melt acquires a yield stress (Bingham-Herschel
   law) and organises a coherent toroidal flow in the intertropical belt |φ| < 30°,
   the Coherent Magmatic Torus (CMT).
2. **Mechanical transition** — the CMT crosses the instability threshold and produces two to
   three episodes of cohesive ejection beyond the Roche radius.
3. **Magnetic transition** — the terrestrial dynamo emerges ≈ 300 Myr after the ejections cease.

**Central equation:**
```
M_ej^max ≈ 2Ω sin ε · U_crit · M_CMT / g_eff(0) ≈ 2.2–4.0 × 10²² kg per event
```

With N = 2–3 and a capture efficiency f_cap ≈ 0.70, the lunar mass is recovered.
Each episode leaves a detectable stratigraphic signature: seismic interface between 200 and
315 km depth, Fe/Si gradient at depth, near-side/far-side crustal dichotomy.

An **interactive HTML5/WebGL simulation** of the TPT dynamics is included in this repository
(open in any browser, no installation required).

## Falsifiable predictions

| ID | Prediction | Mission | Timeline |
|----|-----------|---------|----------|
| **P1** | Seismic impedance contrast \|R\| > 0.02 at depth 267–314 km | Chang'e 7 | August 2026 |
| **P2** | Seismic asymmetry near side vs far side | Multi-station network | 2026–2030 |
| **P3** | Increasing Fe/Si ratio with depth; SPA basin exposes Fe-rich mantle | Chang'e 6, Artemis III | 2026–2029 |
| **P4** | Dynamo delay 290–360 Myr after last ejection | Jack Hills zircons | Ongoing |
| **P5** | Instability only for obliquity ε > 45° | N-body simulations | 1–3 yr |

**Nearest validation window: Chang'e 7 (seismometer, South Pole, August 2026)**
