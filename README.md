## Abstract

The origin of the Moon remains one of the open questions of planetary science.
The canonical giant impact model (Theia collision) predicts neither the near-isotopic identity of Earth and Moon,
nor the crustal dichotomy, nor the ≈ 300 Myr delay of the terrestrial dynamo.
The synestia model faces the same limitations.
This work is conceptually distinct from both: it requires no external impactor, no synestia,
and no free parameter — only the inevitable physical consequences of terrestrial accretion.

The unique driving force is the progressive segregation of Fe-Ni, which orchestrates three coupled
regime transitions — rheological, mechanical, and magnetic — constituting the **Triple Phase Transition (TPT)**.
Elliptical parametric resonance (Malkus 1968; Kerswell 2002; Lacaze et al. 2004) brings the
Coherent Magmatic Torus to bifurcation velocity *U*_crit ≈ 9.8 km/s in ≈ 32 hours,
producing N = 2–3 cohesive ejection episodes in 3–4 weeks and a Hadean dynamo delay of 290–360 Myr.

The model formulates **ten quantitative and falsifiable predictions**.
The central prediction — seismic interface at *d* ≈ 200–315 km, impedance contrast |R| ∈ [0.01; 0.04] —
is testable by **Chang'e 7** (lunar south pole, August 2026) and **Artemis III** (2028–2029).

---

## The three coupled transitions

1. **Rheological** — the silicate melt acquires a yield stress (Bingham-Herschel law)
   and organises a coherent toroidal flow in the Hadean intertropical belt |φ| < 30°
   in the co-rotating body frame (not the geographic equator): the CMT.

2. **Mechanical** — elliptical parametric resonance drives the CMT to bifurcation velocity,
   producing 2–3 cohesive ejection episodes beyond the Roche radius in 3–4 weeks.
   The CMT mass (*M*_TMC ≈ 2.3 × 10²² kg) is internally constrained by the observed
   radius of the first accretion layer (*R*₁ ≈ 1,560 km).

3. **Magnetic** — the terrestrial dynamo emerges ≈ 300 Myr after the ejections cease,
   consistent with Jack Hills zircon palaeomagnetic data.
